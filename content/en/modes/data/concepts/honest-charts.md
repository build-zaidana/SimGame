Charts help people understand quickly. But charts can also **mislead**, on purpose or by accident. Before a report goes out, check four things:

1. **Y axis**: a bar chart must start at **0**. If it starts at 950, a 2% rise can look like it doubled.
2. **Sample size**: "most customers love chocolate" from a survey of 6 people cannot be trusted. The smaller the sample, the bigger the chance of a fluke.
3. **Correlation ≠ causation**: umbrella and hot tea sales both rise in the rainy season. Umbrellas do not make people buy tea.
4. **Time range**: showing only the good months (_cherry-picking_) makes a trend look better than reality.

If a chart has a problem, ask for a **revision** with a clear reason. If it is honest, **approve** it: holding back good reports hurts the team too.
